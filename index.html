Optometric Billing & Coding Academy

A comprehensive, beginner-friendly clinical primer explaining evaluation codes, medical decision making, modifiers, and audit defense for optometrists.

1. E/M Codes (992XX) vs. Eye Codes (920XX)

E/M Codes (99202–99215)

Governed by AMA/CMS 2021/2026 guidelines. Scored purely by Medical Decision Making (MDM) OR Total Provider Time on the date of encounter.

  • 99213: Low MDM. 1 stable chronic illness or 1 acute uncomplicated illness.
  • 99214: Moderate MDM. 1 chronic illness progressing OR prescription drug management (e.g. initiating, refilling, or adjusting glaucoma/dry eye drops).
  • 99215: High MDM. Acute threat to vision or life (e.g. retinal tear, stroke risk, acute angle closure).
Eye Codes (92002–92014)

Unique to ophthalmology and optometry. Not scored by MDM, but by specific mandatory exam elements documented in the chart.

  • 92004 / 92014 (Comprehensive): Complete examination of both eyes, dilated fundus exam, visual fields screening, motility, and initiation of a diagnostic/treatment program.
  • 92002 / 92012 (Intermediate): Evaluation of external eye and ocular adnexa, biomicroscopy, with new or existing treatment plan.
  • Pro-Tip: Some commercial plans reimburse 92014 higher than 99214, whereas Medicare generally reimburses 99214 higher.

2. The Ophthalmic Modifier Decoder

-25 (Significant, Separate E/M)

Appended to an evaluation code (e.g. 99213-25) when a distinct clinical exam is performed on the same date as a minor surgical procedure (e.g. 65222 Foreign Body Removal or 68761 Plugs).

-59 / -XU (Distinct Procedural Service)

Appended to bypass NCCI bundle edits when two tests are performed on the same day for distinct pathologies (e.g. OCT Macula 92134 for wet AMD and Fundus Photo 92250-59 for peripheral choroidal nevus).

-55 (Postoperative Co-Management Only)

Used when an OD assumes post-op care following surgery (e.g. 66984-55 for cataract). Must include transfer of care dates in Box 19 of the CMS-1500 claim.

-79 (Unrelated Procedure in Global Period)

Used if you perform an in-office procedure (like punctal occlusion) while the patient is still in a 90-day post-op surgical global window from another unrelated surgery.

-E1, -E2, -E3, -E4 (Lid Modifiers)

Mandatory for punctal occlusion (68761): E1 = Upper Right, E2 = Lower Right, E3 = Upper Left, E4 = Lower Left.

-GY (Statutorily Excluded Service)

Appended to routine refractions (92015-GY) when billing Medicare Part B so that Medicare generates an official denial, pushing the balance automatically to secondary insurance.

3. The Four Commandments of Surviving a Clinical Audit

  1. Medical Chief Complaint Rule: You cannot bill a medical plan (Medicare/commercial) with a routine chief complaint such as "needs new glasses" or "annual checkup". Document a medical complaint (e.g. eye irritation, blurred vision, diabetes check) or chart a documented transition from routine to medical.
  2. The 4-Part I&R Mandate: Any diagnostic test (OCT, Visual Field, B-Scan, Photo) requires a dedicated Interpretation & Report in the chart containing: (1) Order with medical necessity, (2) Reliability/quality of scan, (3) Structural findings & comparison to prior baseline, (4) Impact on clinical management plan.
  3. Bilateral Code Watchdog: Fundus photography (92250) and Pachymetry (76514) are inherently bilateral codes. Billing them with -RT/-LT or 2 units triggers an immediate auto-denial and audit flag.
  4. Frequency Cap Discipline: CMS contractors (NGS, Noridian, Novitas) strictly enforce frequency caps on serial OCTs and VFs (typically once every rolling 6 months for stable glaucoma). Documenting clinical progression or treatment failure is required to justify more frequent testing.
